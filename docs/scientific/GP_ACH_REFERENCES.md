# Primary-source inspection log · 20 November 2026

**A.** N Suzuki, T Shibayama, T Mambo, Y Gomi. *Spontaneous Tone in Different Types of Longitudinal Muscle Preparations of Guinea Pig Ileum*. Jpn J Pharmacol 69 (1995), 23–28. [Original PDF](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jphs1951/69/1/69_1_23/_pdf), DOI 10.1254/jjp.69.23. Male animals 350–550 g. Intact segments about 3 cm; mucosa and muscularis mucosa removed in the second group; strips about 2 × 30 mm, cut from opened, mucosa-deprived ileum. 30 mL Krebs, air, 35 °C, 1 h, 0.8 g on segments vs 0.3 g on strips. Eightfold isotonic recording on smoked paper. Fig 3: three prior exposures to 1 µM; increasing **non-cumulative** doses every 15 min; peak height from spontaneous tone normalised to maximum. Linear regression on 20–80% responses for EC50 and 95% limits. General analyses means ± SEM, ANOVA/Dunnett or Student t, P<0.05. No significant EC50 difference reported, not proof of equivalence. Fig 1 is a different repeated-dose protocol (20 min intervals): tone relatively stable in segments but declined in strips, increasing apparent excursion without raising peak level. Do not simulate this with a universal baseline.

**B.** R Rodriguez, R Ventura-Martinez, J Santiago-Mejia, MR Avila-Costa, TI Fortoul. *Altered responsiveness of the guinea-pig isolated ileum to smooth muscle stimulants and to electrical stimulation after in situ ischemia*. Br J Pharmacol 147 (2006), 371–378. [Original full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1616997/), DOI 10.1038/sj.bjp.0706618. Adult males 600–900 g; 2 cm intact segments vs MPLM separated from ~3 cm segments. 30 mL Krebs-bicarbonate with choline chloride, pH 7.4, 37 °C, 95:5 O₂/CO₂, 1 g, FT-03C force transducer. Equilibration 60 min under 10 mL/min superfusion; ACh suitability challenge, electrical stimulation, then non-cumulative ACh 10⁻⁹–10⁻⁴ M. 0.3 mL saline additions; wash at maximum (~10 s) with 30–60 mL warm solution, superfuse between additions. This wash time is not a measured time constant. Groups include unoperated controls, paired nonischemic and ischemic tissue. Figs 2–3 report maxima, mean ± SEM, n=6–9 preparations, ANOVA/Dunnett. No significant difference reported between control and nonischemic groups; they are still conceptually distinct. No pointwise normal isometric concentration series supplied.

**C.** J Aboulafia, H Capocci, ACM Paiva, TB Paiva. *Sodium-dependence of the non-specific desensitization of the guinea-pig ileum induced by acetylcholine and histamine*. Br J Pharmacol 90 (1987), 347–353. [Publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1476-5381.1987.tb08964.x); inspected [original paper via Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC1916942?pdf=render). Distal 4 cm segments, both sexes 150–200 g; 5 mL Tyrode, air, 37 °C, resting tension 1 g, Narco force transducer; separate isotonic experiments also reported. Default 90 s contact. Fig 1: 5.5 µM ACh, phasic rise, fade and secondary increase during prolonged contact. Figs 2–3: 90 s control, 10 min rest, 12 min pretreatment, wash, 3.5 min rest, 90 s test; recording speed changes. ACh pretreatment decreased phasic and accelerated secondary tonic response. Fig 4: duration-dependent effect on subsequent phasic amplitude, means of 8–12 measurements. Do not fit treatment duration as a contraction time axis.

**D.** FN Fastier, RD Purves, KM Taylor. *Observations on ‘fade’: a complication of the contractile response of smooth muscle to a large dose of an agonist*. Br J Pharmacol 49 (1973), 490–497. [Publisher](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1476-5381.1973.tb17259.x); inspected [original paper via Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/articles/PMC1776498?pdf=render). 5–10 mm ileal strips; 5 mL Krebs-bicarbonate, Ca²⁺ 2 mM, 37 °C, 95:5 O₂/CO₂, 2 g load; isotonic and isometric methods. 0.5 mL rapid injection, doses expressed as free base. Fig 1 randomised ACh doses in nmol, 30 s recordings: small doses need not peak within observation; large ones fade. Fig 3 includes 0.56 µM ACh with/without double wash; Figs 2, 4 and 6 include histamine recordings, not ACh calibration points. Increasing calcium reduced fade and increased sustained contraction. Transient impairment of coupling by depletion of a calcium store is the **authors' proposal**, not an established universal mechanism. Figure-specific numerical ACh range/axis points not extracted.

**F.** T Unno, S-C Kwon, H Okamoto, Y Irie, Y Kato, H Matsuyama, S Komori. *Receptor signaling mechanisms underlying muscarinic agonist-evoked contraction in guinea-pig ileal longitudinal smooth muscle*. Br J Pharmacol 139 (2003), 337–350. [Original full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1573862/), DOI 10.1038/sj.bjp.0705267. Peeled longitudinal muscle, males 300–400 g. Distinct protocols: Fig 1 isotonic contractions in 5 mL Tyrode, 0.4 g, 36–37 °C; paired fura-2 fluorescence and isometric tension in 8 mL solution, normalised to high K⁺; permeabilised tissue and membrane-potential experiments are different again. Voltage-dependent calcium entry and contractile protein Ca²⁺ sensitisation contribute; M2/M3 interaction is discussed as a hypothesis. Preserve separation of upstream signalling, force and instrument response. Fig 1 carbachol potency/slope are **not** ACh parameters.
